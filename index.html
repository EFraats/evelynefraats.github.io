About Me

Hi, I'm Evelyne, a Doctoral Researcher in Social Neuroscience.

In my PhD research, I use concurrent EEG-tDCS to investigate the neurocognitive processes underlying moral decision-making in both civilian and military populations. My work focuses on understanding how brain activity causally relates to the complex decisions people make in morally challenging situations.

Beyond my doctoral research, I am actively involved in science communication. I am a strong advocate for showing the real side of research, not only the goods ☀️ such as publications, awards, and exciting discoveries, but also the bads 🌧️ including insecurities, setbacks, and rejections.

Through openness, vulnerability, and a touch of cat humor, I aim to provide a more authentic view of academic life and scientific research. This philosophy is reflected in #ResearchGoodsBads, the hashtag I developed and use on LinkedIn.

In addition, I enjoy bringing neuroscience to broader audiences through blogs, public engagement activities, and events for children. One of these initiatives is my science workshop The Cat That Makes the Brain Better, which introduces children to neuroscience, the brain, and brain stimulation through storytelling, creativity, and hands-on activities.

Current Research

I started my PhD in 2024 at Ghent University in the Moral & Social Brain Lab under the supervision of Prof. Dr. Emilie A. Caspar.

My doctoral research investigates the neurocognitive mechanisms that support resistance to immoral orders. Using concurrent EEG-tDCS, I examine the causal relationship between prosocial disobedience and neurocognitive processes such as empathy for pain and the sense of agency in civilian and military populations.

My PhD is a collaboration between the Moral & Social Brain Lab (Ghent University, Belgium) and the Leibniz Research Centre for Working Environment and Human Factors (IfADo, Dortmund, Germany).

In addition, I collaborate with the Royal Military Academy (Brussels, Belgium) and the Faculty of Military Sciences (Breda, The Netherlands) to study moral decision-making in military personnel.
